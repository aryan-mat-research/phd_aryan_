Oxide Nanomaterials for Heavy-Metal Adsorption

Synthesis and experimental evaluation of ZnO, Fe₃O₄ and Fe₃O₄@ZnO nanomaterials, including L-cysteine-functionalised variants, for Pb²⁺ and chromium-ion removal from aqueous solutions.

01 / MATERIALS

Oxide nanoparticle synthesis

The material set included ZnO, Fe₃O₄ and Fe₃O₄@ZnO, alongside L-cysteine-functionalised variants. ZnO was prepared by precipitation, Fe₃O₄ by co-precipitation, and the core–shell composite by hydrothermal treatment.

02 / Pb²⁺ ADSORPTION

Lead-ion removal from aqueous solution

Batch adsorption experiments compared the oxide materials and functionalised variants, and examined adsorbent dose, contact time, pH, temperature, equilibrium behaviour and acid regeneration. Residual Pb²⁺ concentration was measured by AAS.

03 / CHROMIUM ADSORPTION

Cr³⁺ and Cr⁶⁺ removal

Additional AAS analysis was performed for chromium-containing solutions using the oxide and functionalised adsorbents. The available Cr⁶⁺ dataset reports 50 mg adsorbent tests at pH 3 and pH 7. The Cr³⁺ worksheet and the 200 mg Cr⁶⁺ entries currently contain no numerical concentration or removal results.

Cr⁶⁺ · 50 mg adsorbent

pH 3 and pH 7

Cr⁶⁺ workbook values: control concentration 55,984.15 ppm at pH 3 and 881.163 ppm at pH 7. The initial concentrations differ substantially, so compare within each pH condition rather than treating pH as an isolated variable. Workbook column headings contain “Pb” labels in chromium tables; these are retained as a source-data issue to verify.

Cr³⁺ and higher-dose Cr⁶⁺ experiments

The Cr³⁺ sheet lists 50 mg tests at pH 3 and 7 but has blank concentration and removal cells. The 200 mg Cr⁶⁺ tables at both pH values are also blank. These datasets are therefore not plotted and no removal values are inferred.

RESEARCH ROLE

My contribution

  1. 01Prepared and studied ZnO, Fe₃O₄ and Fe₃O₄@ZnO oxide nanomaterials, including L-cysteine-functionalised variants.
  2. 02Conducted batch adsorption studies for Pb²⁺ and carried out AAS-based analysis of residual metal concentrations.
  3. 03Evaluated the effects of adsorbent dose, contact time, pH, temperature and regeneration on Pb²⁺ removal.
  4. 04Performed additional AAS-based chromium adsorption analysis for Cr³⁺ and Cr⁶⁺ systems; the available Cr⁶⁺ results were organised by adsorbent and pH.
  5. 05Processed adsorption datasets, calculated removal efficiency and adsorption capacity, and prepared comparative plots for interpretation.
