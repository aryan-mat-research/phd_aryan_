04 / Substitution

One-for-one oxygen-site replacement

The intended defect model replaces one lattice oxygen with one chalcogen atom. Centre and edge sites should be treated as separate structures with explicitly identified oxygen indices.

MoO₃(010) − O + X    (X = S or Se)
Defect family 1

Centre-site substitution

Remove one selected lattice O and insert one S or Se at the chosen site. Relax the structure, then inspect local coordination, Mo–X distances, adjacent Mo–O bonds, and surface reconstruction.

Defect family 2

Edge-site substitution

Perform the same one-for-one replacement at a selected edge oxygen. Document the site, atom index, initial coordinates, and any fixed atoms so the centre/edge comparison is reproducible.

Composition and geometry checks

  • Confirm the original oxygen is removed; it must not remain alongside the inserted chalcogen in a one-for-one substitution model.
  • Check atom counts, composition, cell vectors, and the mapping between pristine and defect structures.
  • Record the final total energy and force convergence from the complete output.
  • Compare local bonding only after all candidate structures have been optimized under compatible settings.
The existence of a candidate input file does not establish that its geometry converged or that substitution is energetically favourable. Those claims require the final output and a defined reference reaction.
05 / Energetics

Thermodynamic definitions

Use balanced reference states and state the sign convention. Adsorption energy and substitution formation energy describe different processes and should not be conflated.

Molecular adsorption energy

Eads = Eslab+mol − Eslab − Emol

Here, the combined slab–molecule energy is compared with the clean slab and isolated molecule. With this convention, a negative value indicates a lower total energy for the combined system relative to the separated references.

Substitution formation energy

Esub = Edefect − Eclean + μO − μX

Edefect and Eclean are total energies of the substituted and pristine slabs. μO and μX are chemical potentials of the removed oxygen and incorporated chalcogen. The reservoir choices and their reference calculations must be explicitly stated.

Reference consistency

Keep functional, pseudopotentials, cutoffs, k-point sampling, spin treatment, cell setup, and convergence thresholds consistent wherever the energy expression requires a direct comparison.

Interpretation boundary

A favourable adsorption or substitution energy is a thermodynamic statement. It does not demonstrate that the process is kinetically accessible; that requires a converged reaction pathway and barrier.

06 / NEB

Candidate incorporation pathway

The next computational stage is a minimum-energy path connecting a relaxed adsorbed initial state to a relaxed substitutional final state.

Endpoint 1

Initial state

Relaxed adsorption configuration with defined composition, cell, coordinates, and residual forces.

Path construction

Intermediate images

Use a consistent atom mapping and identical cell vectors/composition. Converge NEB forces; refine the saddle region with climbing-image NEB if appropriate.

Endpoint 2

Final state

Relaxed substitutional configuration with the same atom inventory as the initial state.

Ea = ETS − Einitial

The activation energy is measured from the initial state to a verified transition-state region on the converged path. An unconverged highest-energy image is not sufficient evidence of a transition state.
